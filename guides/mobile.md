# Mobile

## Mobile responsiveness: decision map

Treat these CSS-pixel values as starting tokens to test, not universal laws. Keep each product's identity and content hierarchy; do not shrink everything indiscriminately. Use a viewport meta tag and allow pinch zoom. Choose breakpoints when content stops fitting; start testing at 320, 360, 390, 480, 768 and desktop widths, in portrait and landscape.

- **Width and spacing:** At phone widths use 12–16 px side gutters (about 92–94% usable width at 390 px); accommodate safe-area insets where relevant. Prefer fluid widths, `min-width: 0`, and one content column. Use 12–16 px within compact panels and 16–24 px between major groups. Never hide page overflow to conceal a broken layout. Long words and labels should wrap.
- **Navigation:** When a persistent sidebar leaves too little room for content, replace it with a labelled menu button fixed at the bottom right and a right-hand overlay drawer, unless the product or reading direction calls for a different placement. Make the target at least 44 × 44 px, respect safe areas, and reserve content space so the button does not cover important controls. Start at 280 px or 85% of viewport width, whichever is smaller, and full dynamic viewport height; allow the menu itself to scroll. Preserve the product palette, navigation icons, active state and account actions. At the bottom of the drawer, stack the account identity and sign-out action vertically at full available width; keep the avatar and name on one aligned row, allow the name to wrap naturally, and never squeeze the account and sign-out side by side. Opening adds a dim backdrop, locks background scroll, moves focus into the drawer, traps keyboard focus, and exposes expanded state; close by its button, backdrop, Escape or selecting a destination, restoring focus when appropriate. Restore desktop navigation and focusability on resize. Use a restrained 150–200 ms slide/fade, with immediate state under reduced motion. For a few stable top-level destinations, a labelled bottom navigation may be better; avoid adding search without a real need.
- **Typography:** Use body text around 16 px/1.4–1.6, secondary and labels 14 px/1.3–1.5, and short headings about 24–30 px/1.15–1.3 as starting points; preserve readability, importance and contrast instead of scaling every label down. Input text at 16 px also avoids unwanted zoom on some mobile browsers. Let text wrap and support user text enlargement and 200% zoom. Use normal text contrast of at least 4.5:1; do not make essential information tiny or faint.
- **Controls and forms:** Give touch targets at least 44 × 44 CSS px as a practical web target, with sufficient separation; keep the visible icon proportionate. WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum has specific exceptions and is not a recommendation to make common touch controls that small. Stack fields when two columns cannot fit comfortably, keep related labels, errors and inputs together, and use appropriate input types and autocomplete. Keep popovers, calendars and dialogs in view, scroll their own content when necessary, and avoid clipping by ancestors. Respect safe areas and the on-screen keyboard.
- **Dense content:** Stack metric groups and panels when needed; wrap or stack toolbars instead of crushing controls. Keep chart labels readable and reduce tick density before shrinking text. For genuinely two-dimensional tables, boards and charts, use a clearly discoverable, keyboard-accessible local horizontal scroller; preserve row/header relationships. The page itself should reflow at 320 CSS px except content that genuinely requires two-dimensional layout. Do not remove information just to fit.
- **Mobile verification:** Test every page and meaningful menu, form, dialog, filter, chart and table at the widths above, including long content, empty/error states, keyboard focus, touch, zoom, reduced motion and orientation. Check for document-level horizontal overflow, clipped controls, readable actual rendered text, 44 px targets, safe-area overlap, and focus/scroll restoration. Test on real iOS and Android devices where possible; emulation alone cannot validate virtual keyboards, safe areas or touch behavior.

References: [Apple layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), [Apple touch controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/), [GOV.UK responsive type scale](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale/), [GOV.UK spacing](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/spacing/), [WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/reflow), [WCAG target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum), and [MDN modal behavior](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Attributes/aria-modal).


**Mobile reflow decision process:** Inspect each row, grid, card, toolbar and navigation group at its actual available width, including translations, long values and 200% zoom. First preserve the meaning and relationship of the content. If items fit legibly with their required touch targets, keep the horizontal layout. If they crowd, wrap related items as intact groups using flex-wrap and gap, or stack them vertically; make full-width controls or use a single-column grid when wrapping is not clear. Switch at the width where that component actually stops fitting, not merely at a device label. Do not compress words, dates, icons or labels into narrow fragments or hide them. For a board, use full-width vertically stacked status sections on phones when adjacent columns squeeze cards; retain status labels and a usable way to change status, including without dragging. For intrinsically two-dimensional content such as a data table or chart, preserve relationships and use an explicitly labelled, keyboard-operable local horizontal scroller when reflow would lose meaning. Check the final layout and interactions rather than relying on a generic media query.
