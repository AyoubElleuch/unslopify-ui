# Foundations

## Avoid recurring interface clutter

- **No dots beside text:** Remove small colored circles and decorative dots before or after labels, headings, words, and sentences, including dots added by CSS or embedded markup. Do not use colored punctuation as decoration. Where a chart needs a key, use a readable non-circular marker such as a short line and preserve the relationship between the legend and the chart.
- **Remove filler, not just tiny text:** Review every subtitle, eyebrow, caption, breadcrumb, card description, banner, footer, and dialog header. Delete generic slogans, greetings followed by a sentence that restates the page, decorative category labels, and project/task captions that add no actionable context. Do not keep them by making them larger, hiding them only on mobile, or rewording them into another slogan. For example, remove “Here’s what’s happening today” below a dashboard greeting and “A fresh perspective” below a project name; a dialog does not need a tiny breadcrumb repeating its context. Give a dialog a clear, readable title when needed. Keep useful labels, instructions, errors, dates, units, accessibility names, and user-entered content. Decide by meaning and purpose, not font size or CSS class; do not blindly strip all descriptions.
- **Decorative icon tiles:** Do not place a small icon in a colored square or rounded background on every card or panel, including in its corners. Use an icon when it conveys useful information or identifies an action; a decorative background is not required.
- **Persistent desktop sidebars:** Anchor the sidebar to the viewport throughout document scrolling and extend its background across the full viewport height. A `100vh` sidebar that scrolls away with the page can still leave an empty area underneath. Reserve space for it in the content layout; when its contents overflow, scroll them inside the sidebar without clipping controls or shifting the page. Check long pages, short windows, zoom, and responsive navigation.
- **Heading and sentence endings:** End headings and sentences with ordinary punctuation appropriate to the copy, or leave headings unpunctuated. Never replace a period or add an ending with a decorative star, sparkle, sun, colored dot, emoji, or other ornamental symbol. Preserve meaningful punctuation and symbols in user-provided content. Check rendered headings, generated text, and CSS pseudo-elements across every page.
- **Icons:** Give each sidebar navigation destination a meaningful, consistent SVG icon; create or retrieve appropriate icons and size and align their artwork consistently. For other interface icons, make a deliberate decision for each icon: keep it when it clarifies meaning, supports an action, or provides a considered visual benefit. Avoid repetition and remove separate decorative icon backgrounds, borders, and tiles; functional button and selected-row backgrounds can remain. Use consistent SVGs, start at roughly the adjacent text size or line height (typically 16–20px beside 14–16px interface text), align optically, and keep their aspect ratio. Do not stretch an icon to the height of a multiline block. Keep icon-only controls large enough to activate and give them accessible names; hide decorative or redundant SVGs from screen readers. See [Carbon's icon sizing guidance](https://v10.carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/icons/usage/) and [GOV.UK's icon guidance](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/images/).
- **Icon contrast:** Choose an icon color that stands out against its actual surface, using a suitable palette tone rather than assuming that the fourth or most distant swatch will work. Measure the rendered combination: icons required to understand content need at least 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors, with higher contrast helpful for thin strokes. Test states and themes, and do not rely on color alone. [W3C non-text contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast) explains the requirements and exceptions.

## Layout alignment decision map

1. **Identify the shared structure.** Group related elements and establish a common container, grid and consistent inner edges. Align repeated headings, rows, fields, cards, labels and actions to the relevant shared left and right edges; don't align unrelated elements solely to force symmetry.
2. **Choose an anchor for each role.** Align text blocks by their reading edge; align controls in a row by their vertical centers, or text baselines when that gives a better result. Align repeated trailing actions, counts and chevrons to the same visual right edge, including section headers and rows. Use grid columns or flex alignment and shared padding tokens, not independent offsets for every item. In right-to-left layouts use logical start/end edges.
3. **Separate artwork from its hit area.** Preserve a usable target around an icon while positioning the visible mark to the shared alignment line. Equal bounding boxes do not always produce equal visual alignment: account for SVG viewBox whitespace, numerals, asymmetric shapes and font metrics, then adjust optically without moving or shrinking the target. Use tabular numerals and align comparable numeric values consistently.
4. **Test the entire layout.** Inspect header, sidebar, content, dialogs, tables and repeated components together at desktop and mobile widths, zoom, long translated labels, different data lengths, and empty/error states. Check left and right edges, vertical centers, baselines, column headers and values, and focus rings; avoid clipping and overlapping. Reflow or wrap when the grid no longer fits instead of squeezing controls. Confirm intentional exceptions with the actual content.

## Product UI sizing system

Use this decision map for dashboards, workspaces and other application screens. Apply the same principle of role-based tokens to landing pages, but allow marketing and editorial pages their own expressive type and layout scale. Values below are project starting points at a 16px root, not universal standards; validate the chosen font and actual content.

1. **Inventory before changing sizes.** Group examples by role across every route: page and section headings, body and metadata, navigation, form fields, filters, buttons, icon actions, tables and cards. Compare adjacent items and repeated components. Select one density for a context; avoid making a page uniformly smaller to fix a few oversized elements.
2. **Define shared tokens and use components.** Start with spacing 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32px. Choose sizes once per role and reuse them across pages, including hover, focus, errors and empty states. Override by documented purpose, not individual screen or arbitrary selectors. Separate the visible icon size from its interactive target.
3. **Map roles to sizes:**

| Role | Starting size for product interfaces |
| --- | --- |
| Page heading | 28–32px / 34–40px, weight 600–700; use larger display type only when the content warrants it. |
| Section heading / item title | 18–20px / 24–28px; 15–16px / 20–24px for item titles. |
| Body / action / input | 14–16px / 20–24px, normal or medium weight; essential values and actions remain readable. |
| Necessary secondary text | 13–14px / 18–20px; use readable contrast, and remove needless labels instead of shrinking them. |
| Standard form controls | 44px minimum height, 12px horizontal inset, 14–16px text. Equalize inputs, custom selects, date pickers and neighboring buttons in the same row. Let controls grow for wrapped or enlarged text. |
| Dense desktop toolbar | 40px minimum height, 12px horizontal inset, 14px text for all neighboring search, filters and actions. Use 44px on coarse pointers; standard forms stay 44px. Align outer boxes and center labels/icons. |
| Icons | 16–20px artwork alongside text; 40px desktop target for standalone actions, 44px on touch when practical. Avoid scaling all icons with their containers. |
| Cards and board columns | Start with 16px interior padding and 12–16px gaps; size based on content and available width. Avoid oversized empty cards; maintain readable content and preserve the ability to scan and drag. |

4. **Resolve width and responsiveness.** Size fields by expected input, filters by their labels, and buttons by content; align height, not necessarily width. Use min-width: 0 on grid/flex children, wrap or stack at narrow widths, and allow menus and text to fit without page overflow. Use horizontal scrolling for inherently wide tables or boards if necessary, keeping labels readable.
5. **Verify rendered results.** Inspect representative pages at desktop, narrow viewport, 200% text resizing and 400% zoom/reflow; check real long labels, localization, focus, errors and coarse pointers. Compare adjacent control bounding boxes and typography, check text and icon contrast, and ensure targets do not overlap. WCAG 2.2 requires a 24px minimum target subject to its exceptions; 44px is a practical touch goal, not a universal WCAG minimum. Fix a role or token where multiple pages diverge; record intentional exceptions.

Sources: [Carbon button sizes and matching groups](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/button/specifications), [Atlassian spacing](https://atlassian.design/foundations/spacing), [Atlassian design tokens](https://atlassian.design/foundations/design-tokens), [Atlassian typography roles](https://atlassian.design/foundations/typography/applying-typography), [GOV.UK type scale](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale/), and [WCAG target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum). These systems differ; the numbers above are this project's choices, informed by their guidance.

## Spacing, padding, and margins

Use spacing to show relationships, not to fill the screen. Items that belong together should generally be closer than separate groups. Pick a small scale and apply it consistently, then adjust for content, density, and screen size. The values below are **starting points**, not universal rules. Atlassian uses an [8 px-based spacing scale](https://atlassian.design/foundations/spacing); [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/spacing/) uses a different scale and adapts larger spaces for small screens.

- **Start with a scale:** Use `4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64` CSS pixels as a practical set of tokens. Smaller values can be useful for fine alignment. Define tokens once; avoid arbitrary one-off spacing unless the component needs it. Use `rem` where it makes sense for text-relative spacing, and verify actual behavior when text is enlarged.
- **Padding, margin, and gap:** Padding is the space inside a component, between its content and edge. Margin is outside an element. `gap` is usually the clearest way to space siblings in flex or grid layouts. Put outer spacing on the parent when possible, so reusable components don't add unpredictable extra margins. Avoid stacking margins and `gap` accidentally.
- **Buttons:** Start with approximately `10–12px` vertical and `16–24px` horizontal padding for a standard text button; use about `8px` between an icon and label. Allow labels to fit without touching the edge, and keep equivalent buttons consistent. Use `min-height` rather than a fixed height so translated or enlarged text can wrap. Compact controls can use a smaller visual treatment without shrinking the usable target unnecessarily.
- **Target size:** WCAG 2.2 AA generally requires pointer targets to fit a `24 × 24` CSS-pixel square or meet an exception, including its specific spacing exception. Aiming for roughly `44 × 44px` for standalone touch controls is a practical, more forgiving target, not the WCAG AA minimum; a rounded shape must still be evaluated as an actual target. Inline links and other specified exceptions differ. See [W3C target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum) and [Apple's touch guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/).
- **Fields and labels:** Start with `12–16px` horizontal padding inside text fields, and sufficient vertical padding for the text and focus indicator. Keep labels close to their fields (about `4–8px`), error messages close to the field they explain, and separate distinct field groups by roughly `16–24px`. Avoid forcing padding into checkbox/radio or hidden input elements. Make sure custom dropdowns and date pickers have the same care.
- **Cards and panels:** Start around `16–24px` of internal padding, with a consistent gap between title, content, and actions. Smaller dense dashboard cards may need less, while editorial content may need more. Align related content edges; use spacing and structure instead of surrounding every element with another box.
- **Page and sections:** Start with `16–24px` side gutters on narrow screens and `24–48px` on larger screens. Use approximately `24–48px` between major groups where appropriate. A dense data table and a spacious landing page need different rhythms. Limit line length and content width rather than stretching every panel across a large monitor.
- **Navigation and sidebars:** Keep item labels and icons aligned with consistent row padding and a modest icon gap. Separate groups more than individual items. Keep the sidebar within the viewport and scroll overflowing content inside it; retain enough target area and a visible focus indicator.
- **Tables and lists:** Use consistent row and cell padding, for example `8–12px` vertically and `12–16px` horizontally. Keep column headings aligned with their data, preserve enough room for scanning, and allow horizontal overflow on small screens when reflow would destroy the table's meaning. Density should match the amount of data and user needs.
- **Dialogs, menus, and overlays:** Provide breathing room around the dialog content, group title and description, and separate form fields and actions. Keep menu options large enough to activate; leave room for focus rings, error text, and scrollable content. Constrain overlays to the viewport without clipping controls.
- **Input icons and composite boundaries:** Use a clean, consistent SVG icon only when it helps identify the field; align it vertically, keep it inside the field with roughly 12px outer inset and 8px gap to the text, and avoid tiny glyph substitutes. Search fields and other controls with icons, prefixes, suffixes or shortcuts are one visual unit: hover and focus style the complete outer boundary, while the inner input has no separate visible border or ring. Keep text and icons from overlapping, preserve a generous text area, and test pointer, keyboard, zoom, narrow screens and forced-colors mode.
- **Field focus:** Replace browser-default input outlines with a deliberate, visible focus treatment matched to the product palette. Apply it to the entire perceived control: for a search field with an icon or prefix, style the outer field with `:focus-within`, not just the inner input. Standalone inputs, textareas, and custom select/date triggers need their own equivalent focus state. Start with a 1px border change and a restrained 2px outer ring; transition border and ring over about 120–180ms on focus and blur. Measure the indicator against adjacent colors (at least 3:1 for the visual information needed to identify focus), and don't obscure validation or rely on color alone for errors. Keep a clear keyboard focus indicator on every interactive element, support forced-colors mode with a system-color outline, and remove transitions for reduced motion. Check mouse and keyboard focus, focus on wrapped controls, errors, zoom, clipping, and both themes.
- **Form sizing and floating content:** Within a form, use a consistent control height (start at 44px), typography, inner padding and border-box sizing for text, number, date and select controls; align adjacent controls and let their widths follow the grid and the expected content. Equal columns should be equal; a deliberately wider field is fine when its content needs it. Keep labels and validation attached to their fields. Use shrinkable grid tracks and stack fields when two usable columns no longer fit, rather than squeezing text or creating page-level horizontal scroll.
- **Popover placement:** Test calendars, selects, menus and other anchored panels in their actual dialog, drawer or scrolling container. Keep the panel inside the visible viewport and its clipping/scrolling container with a small edge inset; align or shift it horizontally, open above when there is more room, and limit its height with internal scrolling when necessary. Reposition on resize and relevant scrolling. Do not allow an open panel to create horizontal overflow, hide options or obstruct its trigger. Preserve keyboard access, focus visibility and reduced-motion behavior. Verify both open and closed states at narrow widths, near each edge, at 200% zoom, and with long labels; check that all controls and calendar days remain reachable.
- **Responsive layouts:** Reduce outer gutters and large section gaps on smaller screens, but preserve readable internal padding and usable controls. Let flex layouts wrap or switch to a column and let grids reduce columns when needed. Test long labels, translations, text zoom, narrow screens, and very wide screens rather than assuming the same values work everywhere.
- **Verify rather than guess:** Check that text doesn't touch edges, adjacent controls aren't too close, focus indicators aren't clipped, related items look grouped, and nothing overlaps at different viewport sizes and text scales. Validate actual hit areas and exceptions; whitespace by itself is not proof of accessibility.


### Concrete spacing baseline

Use this as a starting specification for a new web app, then adapt it to the design system, content, input method, and accessibility needs. These are this project's recommended defaults, **not measurements mandated by a standard**. CSS pixel values below assume `box-sizing: border-box`; use logical properties (`padding-block`, `padding-inline`) for different writing directions. `min-height` is preferable to fixed height because labels can wrap and text can be enlarged.

| Element | Baseline | Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact text button | 14px type, 20px line-height, 6px vertical and 12px horizontal padding; 34px minimum height. | For dense desktop toolbars. Provide a larger target where appropriate; don't assume a 34px button is a 44px touch target. |
| Standard text button | 14px type, 20px line-height, 10px vertical and 16px horizontal padding; 44px minimum height. | Default for most app actions and forms. Match the height of adjacent fields. |
| Large text button | 16px type, 24px line-height, 12px vertical and 24px horizontal padding; 48px minimum height. | For prominent actions or touch-first layouts; grows beyond 48px when text, padding, and borders require it. |
| Button icon | 16–20px icon; 8px gap between icon and label. | Center standalone icons inside an approximately 44px target. Do not add a second margin when the parent already uses `gap`. |
| Button group | 8px gap between adjacent buttons. | Use 12px if the buttons are visually large or the layout needs more separation. Keep related buttons at the same size. |
| Text field / dropdown | 14–16px type; 12px vertical and 16px horizontal padding; 44px minimum height. | Account for icons, prefixes, and suffixes; give the text its own space. A control grows if its line height and padding need more room. |
| Label and error | 4–8px between label and field; 4–8px between field and its error. | Group the label, field, and error together. Maintain a visible label, not just a placeholder. |
| Form fields | 16px gap between related field groups; 24px between distinct sections. | Use a single-column layout when side-by-side fields are cramped. |
| Compact card | 16px padding; 8px between closely related elements. | Suitable for dense dashboard content. Add room for long text and controls. |
| Standard card / panel | 24px padding; 8px from heading to supporting text; 16px before a distinct action or content group. | Reduce to 16px padding on narrow screens when necessary. The container's parent controls the gap between cards. |
| Sidebar | Approximately 240–256px wide on desktop; 12–16px horizontal inset for navigation. | Fit the actual labels and layout rather than fixing every product to the same width. On small screens, use a usable responsive navigation pattern. |
| Sidebar / menu item | 44px minimum row height, 12px horizontal padding, 8–12px icon-to-label gap, and 4px between rows. | Indent child items by about 16px, while keeping full usable target areas. Separate navigation groups by 16–24px. |
| Table cell | 8–12px vertical and 12–16px horizontal padding. | Choose a consistent row density. Allow height to grow for wrapped content; horizontally scroll or reflow when needed. |
| Dialog / drawer | 24px inner padding; 16px between related controls; 24px before the action group. | Use 16px padding on narrow screens if needed; keep controls, focus indicators, and scrolling within the viewport. |
| Page container | 16–24px side gutters on mobile; 24–48px on larger screens. | Use a max-width for reading-heavy content; align page sections to a consistent grid. |
| Section spacing | 24–32px between app sections; 48–64px for larger editorial sections. | Use less within a group and more between unrelated groups. Avoid applying marketing-page spacing to dense app screens. |

**How text size affects a button:** For a single-line button without a fixed height, its natural outer height is approximately `line-height + top padding + bottom padding + top border + bottom border`; `min-height` can make it taller. A 14px label with 20px line-height, 10px vertical padding, and a 1px border on each side naturally needs 42px. With `min-height: 44px`, it becomes at least 44px. A 16px label with 24px line-height, 12px vertical padding, and 1px borders needs 50px, even if the minimum is 48px. Longer labels, wrapping, browser zoom, and user text settings can increase the needed size; don't crop them to preserve a fixed height.

**What the sources actually specify:** IBM Carbon defines button heights of 32px, 40px, and 48px for small, medium, and large productive buttons, and recommends matching button sizes to adjacent input sizes; its 24px and larger variants have specific uses. Its left navigation example specifies a 256px panel and 32px submenu rows. Atlassian documents 12px side padding for a text-only button, 4px icon-to-label gaps in its own button design, and 24px horizontal padding for panels. These are system-specific examples, not a single universal formula; this project's baseline intentionally uses larger targets and gaps in many places. See [Carbon button specifications](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/button/specifications), [Carbon button usage](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/button/usage/), [Carbon side navigation](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/ui-shell-left-panel/specifications), [Atlassian button spacing](https://atlassian.design/whats-new/evolving-button-and-links), and [Atlassian panel padding](https://atlassian.design/components/panel/usage).
